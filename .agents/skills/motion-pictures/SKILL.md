# Motion Pictures

Design time as deliberately as form.

## Process

1. communication objective,
2. audience/context,
3. references,
4. motion DNA,
5. beat sheet,
6. storyboard,
7. style frames,
8. animatic,
9. senior critique,
10. production,
11. playback review,
12. refinement,
13. delivery.

## Motion DNA

Define:
- pace,
- energy,
- easing character,
- camera behavior,
- transition logic,
- typography behavior,
- spatial depth,
- cut rhythm,
- sound relationship,
- what motion must never feel like.

Examples:
- precise and mechanical,
- soft and continuous,
- energetic but not chaotic,
- cinematic but not slow,
- playful without becoming juvenile.

## Beat sheet

Before detailed animation, define the narrative beats:
- what changes,
- what the viewer should understand,
- emotional progression,
- duration,
- transition to next beat.

## Storyboard and style frames

Separate composition from timing.

Use storyboards to solve:
- sequence,
- framing,
- camera,
- continuity,
- information order.

Use style frames to solve:
- visual language,
- type,
- materials,
- lighting,
- graphics.

## Animatic

Create a low-cost time prototype before expensive production.

Evaluate:
- pace,
- comprehension,
- rhythm,
- dead time,
- overload,
- emotional arc.

## Accessibility and performance

For interface and web motion:
- support reduced-motion preferences,
- avoid flashing patterns that create risk,
- preserve usability without animation,
- budget performance.

## Final review

Watch at actual speed without scrubbing.

Then inspect frame-by-frame only for craft issues.

Check:
- first-watch comprehension,
- visual continuity,
- timing,
- hierarchy,
- sound dependence,
- legibility,
- final-frame usefulness,
- platform crop/aspect variants.

## Handoff

Deliver masters plus timing/style documentation and platform variants where required.
